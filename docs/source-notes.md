# Source: "Body Checking Fundamentals" - Wayne Blatt, NSDA (YouTube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qQGgKYFZKs4)
Condensed from the transcript Robert pasted (2026-10-27). Timestamps = video time.

- 0:22-0:46 Body CONTACT vs body CHECKING. Contact happens all over the rink, even in non-checking leagues (incidental). Checking = deliberately going in to separate the player from the puck. Introduced around PeeWee / Bantam.
- 0:48-1:08 Awareness: head up, look 360 degrees, know where players and hits are coming from.
- 1:08-1:37 Hockey "third position" (athletic stance): head up, knees bent, shoulders over knees, on the BALLS of the feet. On heels = you go down; too far on toes = hard to stay up. Balls of feet lets you absorb or deliver contact. Power comes from the LEGS.
- 1:40-2:00 Three checking tools: SHOULDER (either side), HIPS, HANDS (both hands together; one hand / swinging = roughing).
- 2:02-2:33 Intro drill: stay low, bump each other. TIMING is huge: come in too early and get caught = you go down. Hit at the same time, push with legs, shoulder-to-shoulder.
- 2:35-3:12 Protect the HEAD: don't lead with your head, never hit opponent's head (head check, possible suspension). Lead with shoulder, head away. Watch follow-through: elbow coming up can hit the head.
- 3:13-3:26 Control the STICK: keep it low and out of the way (else high-sticking).
- 3:28-4:07 "Bump" drill: skate forward/backward, on each whistle/"bump" call partners bump. Goal: get comfortable with contact, not big hits.
- 4:14-5:11 Hit in FRONT. Purpose of contact = separate player from puck. Target hands and stick with shoulder; take him out of the play and get the puck moving. Bump-off along boards: hit shoulder, slow him, continue to puck.
- 5:14-5:53 Hitting and PINNING (D zone): NEVER hit from behind. If you can see the numbers on the back of the jersey, don't hit (you can pin). Side by side: shoulder, turn body into boards, drive knee between his feet so he can't get out, so a teammate can come help.
- 5:53-6:53 Open-ice hits and ANGLING: don't go straight at the carrier (gives him options). Come in on an angle to force him one way, then make the hit/play.
- 7:01-8:08 Open-ice TIMING: don't go 100 mph. If he cuts back, do NOT stick out a knee, leg or arm (knee-on-knee injuries, penalties). Angle and time it properly instead.
- 8:11-9:46 Defenseman 1-on-1 (skating backward): line up inside (board-side) shoulder to his shoulder, stay just off his shoulder, ride him to the wall, stick in the middle so he can't cut inside. Don't chase the hit or be over-aggressive (he'll go around you). Be patient, let it come. Goal is separation, not knocking him down.
- 9:52-10:25 DANGER ZONE: about 3 feet from the boards all around the rink (half a stick to a stick length off the boards). Getting hit there = slammed into boards. Two options: get OUT (cut to the middle) or get TIGHT to the boards.
- 10:27-11:31 Puck carrier: keep FEET MOVING; don't glide like a "sitting duck." You can counter-hit / push back even with the puck. Feet moving + tight to boards = you'll be fine and may keep possession.
- 11:35-12:23 Receiving contact: you can always counter/bump back. Players get hurt when they freeze or OPEN UP (turn shoulder/back). Stay aware, good position, feet moving.
- 12:25-13:02 Recap: contact vs checking; checking is a hockey skill; only way to improve is practice (even at home with parents/siblings, giving and taking contact).
